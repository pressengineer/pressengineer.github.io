WHY I WANT TO REPORT

A problem I couldn't look away from

I first started paying close attention to the situation of human rights in North Korea, and to the Japanese citizens abducted decades ago who are still waiting to come home, through readings and reports in my coursework. What struck me wasn't just the scale of the issue — it was how easily it fades from public attention once the headlines move on, while the families involved keep waiting.

That gap — between how serious a story is and how little sustained attention it gets — is what pushed me toward journalism. I'm still at the very beginning of that path. I don't have bylines or published reporting yet. What I do have is a genuine, specific reason for wanting to be there, and a set of technical tools most journalists my age don't start with.

MY ANGLE

Reporting backed by large-scale data and AI

Studying informatics gives me a way into journalism that isn't just writing — it's the ability to process, verify, and find patterns in information at a scale a single reporter usually can't.
